Smoother. A mouse wheel glides like Chrome's: each notch eases over 200 ms from the speed the page already has, so a quick spin is one steady glide instead of a surge per notch that felt like 15 frames a second. The folded sidebar is kept ready just past the window's edge, so sliding it out no longer rebuilds every row: the page behind it no longer drops frames, and SearchX's longest pause while it slides fell from 33 ms to 17 ms. Switching tabs no longer redraws every window and rebuilds the menu bar each time; the menus catch up once you stop. The Hide Elements picker and the Chrome Web Store button load only where they are used, instead of on every page. Privacy: WebKit's fingerprinting protection, the one Safari uses, is on in private windows and can be turned on for everything in Settings › Privacy. A site that breaks can be put in Compatibility Mode from its name in the address field, which sets blocking, fingerprinting protection and extensions aside for that site alone. Pinned tabs can now sleep too (Settings › Tabs).

Tabs and groups move by dragging in the sidebar: a tab up and down, into a group, out of one, or between two, and a group above or below another, its tabs folded under it while it moves. A tab's menu acts on the tab you opened it on, or on every chosen tab when it is one of them, so Unload Tab puts that tab or pin to sleep. Shortcuts follow Safari and Chrome: ⌘D bookmarks the page, ⇧⌘D duplicates the tab, ⇧⌘B shows the bookmarks bar, ⇧⌘W closes the window, ⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ move between tabs and ⌘. stops a page loading. A sidebar button beside the window's buttons does what ⌘S does.

The sidebar slides out as one piece: pinned tabs and rows no longer drift in on their own animations behind it. Choosing a group's emoji keeps the folded sidebar open while the emoji picker is up. The window's close, minimise and zoom buttons are the system's own in every state, including when SearchX is in the background.

Updates install themselves again: when a new SearchX is out, it downloads in the background, checks it against the release's checksum, and replaces the app you have, ready the next time you open it (or at once with Relaunch now). Before, it only offered the disk image to download and install by hand.

Settings is wider, so every line's buttons sit side by side, and it moves: drag it by its title bar, double-click the title bar to bring it back to the middle. With the sidebar folded away, a small spinner in the page's corner shows a page still loading, and goes when the sidebar comes out. Google's sign-in cards lose the light line round their edge. Pinned tabs no longer vanish when the last window is closed before quitting, and they come back unloaded until you open them. Sign-in pages stop asking for a captcha nearly every time: SearchX no longer hides the passkey API from pages when passkeys are off, which had made it look like a tampered browser. A group's icon is now an emoji picked from the Mac's own emoji viewer, or the first site's icon, in place of a coloured dot. Waking a tab from a sleeping group no longer shows the old page sharp through the sidebar while it loads.

SearchX, Search extended. ⌘K now finds your tabs, bookmarks and history, runs any menu command, opens any page of Settings and runs command chains, several commands under one key. Split view shows up to four pages, side by side, stacked or in a grid. Groups take colours and can be split, put to sleep, bookmarked, pinned, or saved and closed for later. Containers keep work and personal sign-ins apart in the same Space. Web panels keep a site docked beside the page. Focus mode (⇧⌘F) leaves the page and nothing else, and SearchX saves power when macOS Low Power Mode is on. A mouse wheel glides instead of stepping, with LinearMouse and other mouse tools left as you set them. Blocking uses uBlock Origin's lists with pop-up blocking that watches behaviour, the new tab turns any picture into pixel art, and SearchX follows the Mac's language.

The first version. A browser for the Mac with nothing in the way: tabs in a row or down the side, pinned tabs that keep their place, and one field for addresses and searches. Ads are blocked before they load; passwords and passkeys stay in your keychain; anything on a page can be hidden; articles open in a reading mode and videos float. Chrome extensions install from the Chrome Web Store on macOS 15.4 or later. Tabs you haven't looked at for half an hour sleep and give their memory back.
